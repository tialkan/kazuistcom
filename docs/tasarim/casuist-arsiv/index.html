01 / Method

The answer is a chain, not a block of text.

Casuist is a grounding and provenance layer over legal corpora. Its design is organised around four testable mechanisms.

01

Proposition-level grounding

Each proposition is bound to the exact source passage it rests on. The passage opens beside the proposition.

↗02

Controlling versus cited

Authority is assessed for jurisdiction, current force and scope. Cited and controlling sources remain distinct.

↗03

Abstention

When the support threshold is not met, the proposition is withheld and marked unsupported.

↗

Defined boundary

Trust starts with what the system does not do.

Casuist is designed for qualified legal practitioners. It supports review. It does not take over judgement.

  1. 01It is not a decision-making or adjudication system.
  2. 02It does not predict judicial outcomes.
  3. 03It does not replace legal advice or professional responsibility.
  4. 04It is not a general-purpose chat interface.

Short answers to the first questions.

Does Casuist provide legal advice?

No. It is being designed as a decision-support and verification layer for qualified legal professionals.

Does it predict court decisions?

No. Outcome prediction is outside the intended product boundary.

What happens when evidence is weak?

The intended behaviour is to withhold the proposition and mark it unsupported rather than complete it fluently.
