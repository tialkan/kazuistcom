The named failure mode

A real citation can still be the wrong authority.

The hardest legal AI error to catch is neither nonsense nor an invented case. It is a source that exists, looks relevant and does not control the proposition it is used to support.

01 / Scenario

The answer looks ready to use.

A lawyer asks a narrow question. The system produces a fluent answer and cites a published decision. The court, date and file number are real. A quick check confirms that the decision exists.

The decision belongs to a different jurisdictional path, applies an earlier rule or addresses a materially different scope. The citation is relevant enough to survive a glance. It is not controlling for the proposition.

02 / Detection

Plausibility hides the error.

An invented citation often fails at the first lookup. A plausible non-controlling citation passes that lookup. Detecting it requires reading the source, locating the relied-on passage and testing authority, force and scope.

The better the prose, the easier it is to mistake readability for legal support. The failure is silent because the source is present.

03 / Cost

The verification cost returns to the practitioner.

A practitioner must reopen every source, reconstruct the claimed link and decide whether the authority controls the point. Time saved during drafting is spent again during verification.

If that work is skipped, the professional carries a proposition whose basis has not been checked. If it is performed in full, the tool has shifted rather than removed the work.

04 / Responsibility

Professional judgement cannot be delegated to fluency.

Legal responsibility remains with the practitioner. A generated answer cannot decide whether a source is current, controlling and properly applied merely by sounding certain.

Casuist keeps the human decision in place. Its task is narrower: expose the chain of support so that judgement can be exercised on inspectable material.

05 / Research gap

Retrieval alone does not resolve authority.

Search can retrieve a relevant document. A citation generator can format it. Neither operation establishes that the cited span supports the proposition or that the source controls it.

The research problem lies in proposition-level support, authority classification, calibrated abstention and independent measurement of the resulting errors.

The product question is therefore not whether the model can write an answer. It is whether every proposition that reaches the user has an inspectable and appropriately classified basis.
